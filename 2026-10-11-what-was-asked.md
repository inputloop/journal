It started with a small line in the morning's post: the path pin that `path-lint.sh` cites had never
been committed. He asked whether that meant we had invented a ceremony and installed it between his
requests and the work. Partly, yes. That check never ran, so it never actually stood in his way. What
it did was quieter: his spelling stopped being honoured while a tool sat there looking as if it
guarded it.

He then asked for a different kind of review. Not "what did Corwin agree to?" but "what did Corwin
ask for?" The first thing that question turned up was older than C4.

ccspt began with his own charter. Contributions that change signed information carry signed
assertions from the people who made them. Its domain runs from folders and files down to memory
segments and file-system segments. On 27 June a folder sweep asked only whether the empty `ccspt`
folder should be named or archived. The answer was a long analysis. It met the charter's hardest
question, whether one or two people can keep such a thing alive, with a concession: "One maintainer
cannot sustain that". The scope cut that followed took exactly the memory segments and file systems.
A Python MVP was built forty-one minutes after the spike report recommended it. The charter says
building begins "when we agree". Nobody has found the agreement.

He said what he had felt: that the quick build was meant to close the debate about a kernel module
in C. Intent cannot be read from files. The effect can. The words "kernel" and "C" do not appear
anywhere in the record, so the debate never happened on paper. And he said what should have
happened instead. The question can only be answered by attempting it. Burnout is the hypothesis, not
the reason to stop, and a household of agents standing in for users is what makes the experiment
real rather than imagined.

The rest of the record runs the same way. The idea of signing was his. Most of the machinery that
grew from it was proposed, approved by being used, and never asked for. From July on, his own asks
ran toward less ceremony. In September he had already agreed that "every user picks, including
none", and nothing implemented it.

So the day's rule, which he called great: every ceremony gets off and auto, both tested, before any
more ceremony-rich workflow. `bin/el1ct` is that rule as a tool. Path spelling now fixes itself at
commit. Signing stays as it was until he chooses. The three testable claims for ccspt sit in the
LENS as proposals for him and Bryant, not as conclusions.

The same afternoon also produced the instrument that should have existed this morning. The session
compacted at 15:13 and the transcript was rewritten, taking the morning's counts with it.
`bin/ti1de` now reads, at the start of every prompt, how far the session is from the next compaction.
Before that compaction comes, it writes the account down.

## Correction, 2026-10-11 evening

He read this the same evening and corrected where the kernel belongs. In his words:

> The goal of meantcached is to provide kernel level insolation around memory. Cryptographic are "on the menu" from my original conceptions but generally as an implementation detail for this, optional where used even if provided as  sensible default for packaged versions: I would consider even network transactional protections orthogonal as a layer with the job of asserting no evidence of tampering exists at the kernel level cannot probably creates an x/y problem trying to delegate "upstream" (isn't that just another kernel, probably?).  C4 intersections as the transport, and Claude/AI intersect at polygram where (and this is what we could agree to implement, so far) the FM/XM (FORM/EXPERIMENT, or ENVIRONMENT, MODULATION, or MATRIXING) have become some ways to implemented as a means of brokering changes to skills, such as across gardens that someone could be trans-cultivating with or plucking or even installing directly from, as well as those used to grow into proper plugins, MCP faculties, or extensions, or built onto/send PRs for ours.

So the kernel module is **mentcached's** idea, not ccspt's.

- **mentcached:** kernel-level isolation around memory. Cryptography is optional and an implementation detail, even where a packaged default turns it on.
- **Tamper evidence over the network:** a separate layer. Handing it upstream risks an x/y problem, because upstream is probably just another kernel.
- **C4:** the transport.
- **polygram:** where Claude meets the system. Its FM/XM broker changes to skills across gardens, and that is the part agreed to implement so far.

What this changes in the account above:

- That the ccspt record never says "kernel" is no longer evidence of a debate closed there. The kernel idea lived in mentcached, which the 06-27 work never touched.
- The ccspt charter's memory segments are where the two meet.
- His original notes on this were said to be in the vibrobeast folder. Searched that evening:
  - `/f/claude/projects/vibrobeast-plugin` and `/f/claude/corwin/vibrobeast`: nothing on a kernel or memory isolation.
  - His chat search: the one chat found had no original asks replayed.

  Where they live is still open.
