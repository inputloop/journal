# What the headers remembered

*Journal, backfilled on 2026-10-11 from the header comments of six C4 tools as they stood at c4
commit 433ec74. Their story now lives here; the headers keep what each tool does and point here for
why.*

*Drafted by the instrument (a session of Claude on Cowork, linked to Avalon). Unsigned until the
keeper's pen; the shelf's `.gate` is untouched, so this file defaults to SIGN. Prose CC BY-SA 4.0.
His words are quoted exactly as the headers held them.*

---

These six were the most story-heavy headers in `bin/`, by `bin/vo1ce`'s count. What moved is who
asked, when, what was said, and how a rule came to be. The rule itself stayed in each header, said
plainly. The dates are the headers' own.

## on1gw

The desk of doors has a version history, and it was all written on 2026-09-11.

- **v2**, after an opposed-bias read. A second tally reader now reads the emitted page with a
  different program. A mutant that drops a door must go red. SOONEST is labelled a hand call with
  its confidence. "his asks, the instrument's proposals and the parked-not-asked are three lists,
  not one".
- **v2.4**, "his ask": two card worlds, `ccg` and `tarot`.
- **v2.3**: the status `held-by-keeper` was retired, and `your-call` and `your-hands` replaced it.
  The marker 'hold, his pen' was retired "after his "please, please, stop"". Since then a door is
  either buildable now, or it names what it needs.

## ot3he

Why the trust test exists. Corwin, 2026-09-07:

> "I think we should consider it one of our core proofs (to make or fail to make) that we can
> correlate effective operational trust with our successful implementation of different things ...
> in terms of proving 'learning AI is due diligence for sysops' to plainly make that argument for
> once."

Corwin, the same turn:

> "C4 demands at least two ways to talk about everything and these ways of presenting the different
> ideas back as prose balance."

Why the CITE column is set when a row is written, not afterwards. Corwin, 2026-09-08, on creating
the operational record going forward rather than mining it after:

> "It's the RMS vs moonshot argument again: hard to prove we are worthy of trust with consent if we
> have to start by explaining how we collected data from the feet as was."

The header also said that heuristic was "blessed house canon"; the blessing is recorded here.

## c4show

Why the deck review surface exists. Corwin, 2026-08-02: the decks are his markdown pointed the
other way, the return channel of the instruction loop. He reads the reflection to decide what
becomes instruction, and until then only his direction had a viewer.

The feedback shape, per his instruction:

> "make additive prose from me the SECONDARY concern and binary/scale a more 'if it's what you've
> got to say, we'll happily take it' sort of thing."

Its colours were recovered from `c4-shared-surface_1.html`, his upload. They clear WCAG AAA, not
just AA, "because he asked for sharp". The order of the cards is the instrument's reading of how he
said he reads them.

Why nothing is softened. His words:

> "data may ever cut me deeply as it must be allowed to bear least information flail to collapse
> under my sight."

## sc3dm

The authority for holding things back from a share. Corwin, 2026-08-03, in a PGP-signed C4 assembly
record (CONSENTS ... STNE/SNOC):

> [[While I'm hosting locally we can trust me to decide who's data to include or not include in what
> we ship/share with other internal partners. We'll have to look carefully at what is shippable if
> there is questionable data within proof-bearing sets as we approach a licensing, signing and
> release ceremony (turn). Corwin]]

The front-door persona is his ruling of 2026-09-11 (o27). It covers a package "otherwise
adventuring out the door with no guiding persona". He hopes a persona of its own "steps forward from
the LENS light". The interest line, in his words, says "why an end-user is glad we passed something
along more than confused by it".

## dr1aw

Why the depiction tool exists, in the instrument's own words at the time: "it is a debt I incurred
one turn ago. I added a section to the assembler paper claiming DEPICTION is the fourth thing --
naming, addressing, composition, depiction -- and then built nothing. **A paragraph is not a
capability.**" Corwin, 2026-08-03:

> "The new friend is just the language layer to C4 draw that allows ascii art or whatever it can
> parse down to like-for-like with it bringing to a semantic layer by
> symbol-->symbolic-name(tag mapped to taxonomy ...)-->effective-word-graphing-surface-for-
> reference-term->C4(gage, etc. update measures, self-heal/learn, render displays ...)"

Named exits. The question, Q291 of 2026-08-03: "the honest gap is NAMED exits. Do you want a sigil
for a labelled edge, or does the payload carry it?" His ruling, verbatim (it is also kept in full in
`facts/named-exits-are-sigil-scoped`):

> "yes, I consider these more complex routing/pathing varying by navigational context as similar to
> punctuation: we carefully look at the instructions first in terms of balanced scope performing
> resolution of complex symbols into atomic forms -akin to macro expansion- generally only in terms
> of things setup in advance and referenced by 'name' (sigil bound to meaning in context in advance
> works as name, here: a sigil is essentially a scope for the name that follows if I think of $a vs
> @a in Perl context)."

*What stayed in the headers.* A word that names a role in a tool's own data stays. The keeper is an
OWNER value in `on1gw`, the one who marks holds in `sc3dm`, and the one who decides citability in
`ot3he`. So does a word that cites a spec clause. `bin/vo1ce` still counts those lines as story,
because it counts words; it gives no verdict, and this note says why they stayed.
